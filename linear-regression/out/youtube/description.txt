You want to sell your 1,800 sq ft house. One agent says $260,000, another says $200,000. Five houses on your street sold last year, and a ruler line through them gives a different price for everyone who draws it. Linear regression finds the one best line, and here the fair price comes out at $230,000. A 16-minute explainer for people who don't think of themselves as "math people".

🌍 WHERE YOU'LL USE IT
• Machine Learning / ML (12:25): linear regression is the first model you train: the score is the loss, training finds the smallest loss
• Data Science / DS (12:43): trend lines, e.g. how much sales rise per extra $1,000 of advertising
• Finance (12:50): a stock's beta is the slope of its moves against the market's moves
• Computer Science / CS (13:01): fit run time against input size; the slope is the cost per item (and predicting huge jobs is extrapolation)

📐 KEY FORMULAS
• Line: y = a + b·x   (y = price, x = size, b = slope, a = intercept)
• Residual (miss) = actual − predicted, measured straight up and down
• Score: sum of squared errors = Σ (miss)²   (Σ = add them all up)
• Slope: b = Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) ÷ Σ(x − x̄)²   (x̄, ȳ = the averages)
• Intercept: a = ȳ − b·x̄   (the best line always passes through the average point)

✅ WORKED EXAMPLE
Five sales: 1,000 sq ft $140k · 1,500 $220k · 2,000 $250k · 2,500 $280k · 3,000 $360k
• Average house: 2,000 sq ft, $250k
• Size in hundreds of sq ft: Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) = 2,500 · Σ(x − x̄)² = 250 → slope 10 ($100 per sq ft)
• Intercept: 250 − 10 × 20 = 50 → price = $50,000 + $100 per sq ft
• Misses: −10, +20, 0, −20, +10 → sum of squared errors 1,000 (in $ thousand²)
• Other lines: flat at the average 26,000 · through the end houses 1,250 · too steep 7,250
• 1,800 sq ft → $230k (give or take about $20k) · 10,000 sq ft → $1.05M is extrapolation
• One outlier ($560k instead of $360k) moves the slope to $180 per sq ft and the 1,800 sq ft price to $254k
(Illustrative house prices, not real sales.)

🧭 THE RECIPE
1. Plot and look: the dots should lean along a line
2. Measure the misses: straight up and down, in the units you predict
3. Square and add: that total is the score (sum of squared errors)
4. Find the best line: slope from the formula, then the intercept from the average point
5. Read and predict: the slope is a rate; predict only inside your data
"A line does not know the truth. It is just the one guess that misses least."

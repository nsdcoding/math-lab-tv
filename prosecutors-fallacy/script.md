## The Formula Behind It  ·  93 words, ~34 s

- **s055** What we just did is count, but you can write it as a short rule.
  - *Visual:* Clean slate; heading 'the counting rule'.
- **s056** The chance of guilt, given a match, equals the guilty people who match, divided by everyone who matches.
  - *Visual:* Formula in words: 'P(guilty | match) = guilty matches ÷ all matches'. Numerator orange, denominator warn.
- **s057** On top, one. On the bottom, one plus eight. One ninth. ⏸0.8s
  - *Visual:* Numbers drop in: '1 ÷ (1 + 8) = 1/9' (LaTeX fraction), violet result.
- **s058** In our Bayes theorem video, we wrote this same idea as a formula. Here, counting the crowd gets you to the same answer, with no formula to remember.
  - *Visual:* Chip 'from: Bayes' Theorem'. Small Bayes formula appears, then the 'count the crowd' picture beside it with '=' between.
- **s059** And notice where the eight came from. Not from the expert's number alone. It came from the size of the crowd. ⏸1.0s
  - *Visual:* The '8' in the denominator is circled aqua; arrow back to '8,000,000 people'.

## Common Mistakes  ·  157 words, ~57 s

- **s108** Let's name the mistakes, so you can catch them.
  - *Visual:* Heading 'common mistakes' (red).
- **s109** Mistake one: the swap itself. Reading the chance of the evidence, given innocence, as the chance of innocence, given the evidence.
  - *Visual:* Red card ①: 'P(match | innocent) ≠ P(innocent | match)' with the swap arrows.
- **s110** Listen for it in the words. One in a million chance an innocent person would match, is not the same as one in a million chance he's innocent. ⏸1.0s
  - *Visual:* Two speech bubbles: left green ✓ 'an innocent person would match: 1 in a million', right red ✗ 'he is innocent: 1 in a million'.
- **s111** Mistake two: forgetting the crowd. A rare match means very different things in a village and in a whole country.
  - *Visual:* Red card ②: mini slider with '999 in 1,000' ↔ '1 in 61'.
- **s112** Mistake three goes the other way. Some defense lawyers say: there are nine matches, so the chance he did it is only one in nine. The evidence is worthless.
  - *Visual:* Red card ③: speech bubble 'only 1 in 9 → worthless!' with a red ✗.
- **s113** That's a fallacy too. The match took eight million people down to nine. That's powerful evidence. It just isn't proof on its own. ⏸1.0s
